# Pip, the office guide

Pip is a friendly blue-and-white robot who strolls around the main office floor. He pauses and faces you when you approach. He is a personal guide in your browser, so other people can take their own tours independently. He does not edit files, hire workers, or make AI requests.

Press **E** near Pip, press **F2** from anywhere in the 3D view, or click **Ask Pip** on screen. His explanations assume no coding experience. Each tool has a short description, why you might use it, practical steps, and definitions of unfamiliar words. Many tools include a sample instruction you can give a coding worker. Search everyday phrases such as “first task,” “error,” or “pull request.” Pip is a built-in reference guide, not an open-ended AI chat.

**Show me around** starts an eight-stop tour: projects, workers, issues, the task queue, pull requests, previews, collaboration, and break time. Pip follows the office’s navigation grid around furniture and waits at each stop. He also waits if you fall behind. Read the explanation by pressing **E** near him or **F2**, then continue or end the tour. The tour card remains visible while you walk. You choose when to advance; explanations do not pop up over your work automatically.

**Walk me to this tool** is optional: it walks your character to a suitable place near the tool and opens its explanation. Movement keys cancel the walk through the office’s usual controls. On other maps or the roof, you can read the reference but the main-office walking tour is unavailable. Switching floors or maps ends a tour. Closing the guide with its **✕** or **Esc** returns you to the office’s normal controls.

Pip’s explanations cover coding workers, terminals, repositories, GitHub sign-ins, issues, queues, changes, commits, branches, worktrees, pull requests, services and previews, controls, settings, dictation, collaboration, and recreational tools. Pip’s guide does not consume an agent allowance; coding workers themselves can use your subscriptions or API allowance.
